1. Purpose

This module closes the simulated-data workflow by comparing all seven genomic prediction methods on exactly the same outer-assessment individuals defined in M02:

  • GBLUP_A, GBLUP_AD, and GBLUP_ADE;
  • ANN_0, ANN_L1, ANN_L2, and ANN_L1_L2.

Predictive performance is evaluated using the known true simulated genetic value as the primary target. The observed phenotype is a complementary target because it includes residual variation.

The six C1-C6 settings represent simulated genetic scenarios, not six unrelated measured traits. They differ in QTL number and nominal heritability, and model performance is compared separately in each.

The comparison follows three principles:

  • all seven methods use the same 25 outer-assessment tasks per scenario;
  • each task evaluates the same 200 held-out individuals for every method;
  • each of the twelve metrics is plotted separately for C1-C6, without a performance mean that combines all six scenarios.

Because repeated outer-assessment sets overlap, the figures are descriptive and do not treat folds as statistically independent replicates.

3. Evaluation metrics

The seven models are assessed using the same held-out performance measures. For individual \(i\), let \(p_i\) be the prediction, \(t_i\) the evaluation target, and \(n=200\) the size of each assessment set. True genetic value and observed phenotype are evaluated separately.

  • Pearson correlation measures linear association between prediction and target; larger values are better.
  • Spearman correlation measures monotonic rank association between prediction and target; larger values are better.
  • RMSE and MAE measure prediction error on the original target scale; smaller values are better.
  • Bias is mean(prediction - target) and is retained as a complementary calibration measure; values nearer zero are preferable.
  • Slope is the prediction coefficient in lm(target ~ prediction) and is retained as a complementary calibration measure; values nearer one are preferable.
  • No \(R^2\) metric is used.

The prediction errors and bias on the original target scale are \[ \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i-t_i)^2},\qquad \mathrm{MAE}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|p_i-t_i|, \] \[ \mathrm{Bias}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i-t_i). \] The calibration slope is the coefficient of the prediction in \(t_i=\alpha+\beta p_i+\varepsilon_i\). Pearson measures linear association, and Spearman measures rank association. Each metric is calculated within each outer-assessment task before the task-level values are averaged.

For all twelve measures, the figures display 25 task-level observations within each scenario and model. White diamonds show means within individual C1-C6 scenarios, not across scenarios. Bias is assessed relative to zero, and slope relative to one.

4. Comparison against true genetic value by scenario

The known true genetic value is the primary prediction target. All seven methods are compared for each of the six metrics and for each C1-C6 scenario. The simulation configurations, inherited unchanged from M01, are C1 (8 QTL; nominal heritability 0.7), C2 (40; 0.7), C3 (80; 0.5), C4 (120; 0.5), C5 (240; 0.3), and C6 (480; 0.3). QTL counts and nominal heritabilities are simulation inputs, not estimated genetic parameters. Because these settings change together, scenario differences cannot be attributed to one factor in isolation.

Within each panel, boxplots summarize 25 held-out task values, points show each task, and white diamonds mark within-scenario means. Pearson, Spearman, bias, and slope use common metric axes across the panels. RMSE and MAE use scenario-specific horizontal axis ranges to make smaller within-scenario differences visible; compare models within each panel and read its tick labels before comparing error magnitudes across scenarios. Repeated outer splits overlap, so the distributions do not imply independent replication or statistical significance.

6. Scenario-specific interpretation

For each scenario it reports the descriptive leader for the four genetic metrics (Pearson, Spearman, RMSE, and MAE) and for phenotype Pearson and RMSE. These are numerical comparisons under the evaluated simulation conditions, not significance tests or an overall ranking of the seven models.

The remaining phenotype Spearman and MAE plots assess agreement in ranks and absolute error. Bias and calibration slope must be read against their respective reference values of zero and one; they are not measures where the largest raw value is necessarily preferable. 7. Regularization effects among ANN models

ANN-0, ANN-L1, ANN-L2, and ANN-L1+L2 share the architecture and learning rate selected with ANN-0 within each outer task, as established in M04. Their differences therefore describe the evaluated regularization choices conditional on that shared structural selection rule.

Compare the four ANN variants within each C1-C6 panel for the genetic and phenotype metrics. The Pearson and Spearman figures reveal their association with the two evaluation targets, RMSE and MAE show error magnitudes, and bias and slope show calibration differences. The relative effects of L1 and L2 should not be summarized by a mean that pools the six genetic scenarios.

8. Scientific conclusion

The seven genomic prediction methods were evaluated on matched outer tasks in six distinct simulated genetic settings. The twelve reported metrics are shown separately by C1-C6 and by evaluation target, without averaging performance across the six scenarios.

Pearson and Spearman quantify linear and rank association; RMSE and MAE quantify prediction error; bias and slope assess calibration relative to zero and one. Descriptive differences do not establish statistical significance or a universal ranking across simulation settings. The scenario-specific means can optionally be exported for inspection, but no global model performance summary is generated.
