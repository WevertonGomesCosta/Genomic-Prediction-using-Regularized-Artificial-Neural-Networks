Purpose

This module provides the terminal comparison between GBLUP-ADE and the four ANN variants under the frozen outer-CV design. All competing methods are evaluated on identical outer-assessment individuals. Prediction of the true simulated genetic value is the primary outcome, with phenotype prediction retained as a secondary outcome.

Results are summarized overall, by simulation scenario, and through task-matched ANN versus GBLUP-ADE contrasts.

Canonical inputs

This terminal comparison consumes the finalized outputs from the two parallel modeling branches. From the GBLUP-ADE module it reads the 150 task-level result rows and 30,000 held-out predictions; from the ANN module it reads the 600 task-by-model result rows and 120,000 held-out predictions for the four ANN variants.

These inputs are compared under the same frozen outer-CV design established upstream. This module does not fit predictive models, tune hyperparameters, select training duration, or modify the held-out predictions. Its role is to verify cross-method compatibility and then summarize performance on matched tasks and matched outer-assessment individuals.

Compatibility audit

The result-level audit verifies the frozen task design. The prediction-level audit independently verifies that the actual held-out individuals, observed phenotypes, and true simulated genetic values are identical across methods.

The full compatibility audit is retained internally. For interpretation, the fair-comparison contract can be summarized by the four conditions below.

Interpretation of calibration metrics

The same metric definitions are applied separately to prediction of the true simulated genetic value and to prediction of the observed phenotype.

  • Bias is the mean prediction error, calculated as \(\operatorname{mean}(\text{prediction} - \text{target})\). A positive value indicates average overprediction, a negative value indicates average underprediction, and zero is the calibration target.
  • Slope is the coefficient of the prediction term in the regression lm(target ~ prediction). A value near one indicates appropriate proportional scaling of predictions relative to the target. Slope alone is not a complete calibration assessment because the regression intercept also contributes to calibration.
  • R-squared is calculated as \(1 - SSE/SST\), using prediction errors relative to the target mean. It is therefore not defined here as squared correlation. For genuinely held-out predictions it can be negative when the predictions perform worse, in squared-error terms, than using the target mean as a reference.

Correlation, RMSE, and MAE describe complementary aspects of predictive agreement, while Bias and Slope provide calibration diagnostics. No single metric is interpreted as establishing universal model superiority.

Overall conclusion

Within the prespecified canonical model and ANN architecture search space, GBLUP-ADE provides the strongest overall predictive performance and leads the primary genetic-value metrics across all six simulation scenarios. Among the neural networks, ANN-L1+L2 has the best overall averages by a very small margin over ANN-L1, while ANN-L2 remains close to the unregularized ANN.

These results support a specific conclusion for the frozen design evaluated here; they do not establish a universal ranking between genomic mixed models and all possible neural-network architectures.
